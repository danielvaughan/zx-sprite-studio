# Playtest charter: Sprite Studio M6, round 1 (sit-beside trial)

- Tap: `build/sprite-studio-m6.tap` (sha256 in the report: check the loaded sha256 against `sha256sum` of the
  file before playing)
- Report: `playtest/m6-r1.md`; screenshots `playtest/media/m6-r1-*`
- Method: Yvonne Standish's sit-beside trial (`zx-game-ux` §4). You are **a reader of the drawing chapter's
  "From paper to defb" section**: you know a game stores a sprite as rows of bytes, that a cast of sprites is kept
  together, that a Spectrum keeps work on tape (SAVE and LOAD), and that a game often stores a sprite pre-shifted
  to each of 8 positions and both facings, which costs memory. Keys first. You get **only the job cards**.
  Think aloud; 1987 words.
- Speed: 25% or frame-step, stated. Agent-operated; full-speed feel not verified. The harness has no Kempston
  mouse and no tape deck: say so. Saving and loading cannot complete in the harness (no tape to record or play);
  try them as far as the screen lets you, and say what the screen tells you and whether you can get back.

## Job cards

| Job | Goal (the reader's words) | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | Keep two sprites together: the lawn mower and a new one of your own | both are listed together |
| J2 | Draw something small on your new sprite, then go back to the mower, then back to yours | your drawing is still there |
| J3 | Find out how much memory the mower would take in a game, facing one way, at 8 positions | you have the number and can say what it counts |
| J4 | The same if it must face both ways and only sit on whole bytes | you have the new number |
| J5 | Save your sprites to tape (as far as you can here), and find out how to stop | you know how it would save, and you are back |
| J6 | Load sprites from tape (as far as you can here), and stop | the sheet is as it was |
| J7 | Get the mower's bytes out for a game (as far as you can here) | you know what would be written |
| J8 | Try to keep more sprites than the studio allows | you are told plainly |

For each job: finished?, frames, presses, slips, hesitations, think-aloud lines.
